# The Five Hews and Twelve Pieces — Zettel 23–26

<p class="fff-deck">The names of the lessons that follow, gathered into four short verses.</p>

## TL;DR

These verses read like a table of contents: five hews and twelve further principal pieces, named here and explained later. For me, this is one of the passages that suggested reading the *Zettel* as a teaching syllabus.

## 1. Zettel

<div class="fff-original" markdown="1">

<p class="fff-label">Original · Rome witness, Cod. 44.A.8, verses 23–26</p>

> **23** Zorñ häw krumpp twer  
> hat schiler mit schaitlar  
> **24** Alber vorsetzt  
> Nachraisen vberlauff haw setzt  
> **25** Durchwechsel zuck  
> durchlauf abschneid hende druck  
> **26** heng wind mit plössen  
> Slach vach streich stich mit stössen

<p class="fff-source">Witness/transcription: pseudo-Peter von Danzig, Rome <em>Cod. 44.A.8</em>, fol. 12v; Dierk Hagedorn's transcription, as displayed on <a href="https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/Pseudo-Peter_von_Danzig">Wiktenauer</a>.</p>

### Sense translation — provisional, gloss-informed

> **23** Zornhau, Krumphau and Zwerchhau; Schielhau and Scheitelhau.  
> **24** The guards and the displacements; following-after, overrunning, setting aside strikes.  
> **25** Changing through, pulling, running through, slicing off, pressing the hands.  
> **26** Hanging and winding to the openings; striking, catching, sweeping, thrusting and pushing.

</div>
</div>

The rendering of *Alber* and *vorsetzt* as groups rather than individual actions follows the glosses, which identify them as the four guards and four displacements.[^gloss-rdl]

## 2. Historical glosses

<p class="fff-label">Source summaries · attributed editorial wording</p>

**GNM Hs. 3227a** preserves the list of five hews and other fencing pieces without a separate explanation. It proceeds to the Zornhau material.[^gloss-3227a]

**RDL** makes the purpose of the list explicit. The glosses identify the names of the principal pieces so that the reader can recognise them, then enumerate the five hews and the twelve remaining pieces. Ringeck counts seventeen principal pieces in total; pseudo-Danzig and Lew present the same broad organisation as five hews followed by twelve pieces. They are not explained in detail here: the glosses direct the reader to the teachings that follow.[^gloss-rdl]

## 3. My historical interpretation

I do not see much reason to develop a separate technical or modern HEMA interpretation of these verses. They name the subjects; the following sections teach them.

Still, this short passage matters to how I read the whole work. It is one of the things that originally led me to think of the *Zettel* as a **syllabus** and the glosses as the accompanying teaching material. This is not how a fencing exchange is described. It is how the contents of a course might be introduced. I have explained that hypothesis in the [*Preface*](preface.md); here, the list can simply speak for itself.
